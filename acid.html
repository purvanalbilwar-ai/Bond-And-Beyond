Acids & bases · the acid side

What makes an acid?

Chemists describe acids in different ways. Learn the three major definitions, then see how acid strength is classified.

An acid can donate a proton

Hydrogen chloride transfers H⁺ to water. The products are hydronium and chloride ions.

HCl + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻HCl acts as a Brønsted–Lowry acid

Three definitions of an acid

Each definition describes acidic behaviour from a different point of view.

💧

Arrhenius acid

In water

An Arrhenius acid increases the concentration of hydrogen ions, H⁺ (present as H₃O⁺), when dissolved in water.

Example: Hydrogen chloride forms ions in water:
HCl + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻
🔁

Brønsted–Lowry acid

Proton donor

A Brønsted–Lowry acid donates a proton (H⁺) to another substance.

Example: HCl donates H⁺ to water:
HCl + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻
••

Lewis acid

Electron pair acceptor

A Lewis acid accepts a pair of electrons to form a bond. This definition includes reactions without proton transfer.

Example: Boron trifluoride accepts a lone pair from ammonia:
NH₃ + BF₃ → H₃N→BF₃
⚡

Strong and weak acids

Extent of ionisation

Strong acids ionise almost completely in water. Weak acids ionise only partially and establish an equilibrium.

Strong: HCl + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻
Weak: CH₃COOH + H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + CH₃COO⁻
Remember: Strong vs weak describes how much an acid ionises in water. Concentrated vs dilute describes how much acid is present in a given volume; these are different ideas.

How the definitions differ

The definitions are related, but each one focuses on a different part of a reaction.

ArrheniusRaises H₃O⁺ concentration in water.HCl + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻
Brønsted–LowryDonates a proton, H⁺.HCl donates H⁺ to H₂O.

Acid or acidic solution?

An acid is a substance that can donate H⁺ or produce H₃O⁺ in water. An acidic solution contains more H₃O⁺ than OH⁻ ions and has a pH below 7 at 25°C.

Neutralisation

When an acid reacts with a base, they can form a salt and water. The net ionic equation is:

H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂OHCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O

Monoprotic and polyprotic acids

A monoprotic acid can donate one proton per molecule, such as HCl. A polyprotic acid can donate more than one; sulfuric acid, H₂SO₄, is diprotic.

Acid strength and pH

A stronger acid ionises more in water at the same concentration. For dilute aqueous solutions at 25°C, pH + pOH = 14. The pH page shows where solutions sit on the scale.

Examples from school chemistry

These examples are commonly studied in school chemistry.

🧪

Hydrochloric acid

Formula: HCl · a strong monoprotic acid; it ionises in water to form hydronium and chloride ions.

🧪

Nitric acid

Formula: HNO₃ · a strong monoprotic acid used in many school reaction examples.

⚗️

Sulfuric acid

Formula: H₂SO₄ · a diprotic acid; its first ionisation in water is strong.

⚗️

Ethanoic (acetic) acid

Formula: CH₃COOH · a weak acid that only partially ionises in water.

🧪

Carbonic acid

Formula: H₂CO₃ · a weak acid formed when carbon dioxide dissolves in water.

🍋

Citric acid

Formula: C₆H₈O₇ · a weak organic acid found in citrus fruits.

Acids in everyday products

Acids are useful in food, biology, transport, and manufacturing.

🥗

Food and pickling

Ethanoic acid, CH₃COOH, is the acid in vinegar and is used in pickling and food preparation.

🍋

Food and flavour

Citric acid gives many fruits their tart taste and is used as a flavouring and preservative in foods.

🥤

Carbonated drinks

Dissolved carbon dioxide forms a small amount of carbonic acid in fizzy drinks.

🚗

Lead-acid batteries

Sulfuric acid is the electrolyte in lead-acid batteries used in many vehicles. The battery acid is corrosive and should not be handled.

🌱

Fertilisers

Sulfuric and nitric acids are used in making important fertiliser chemicals.

🫀

Digestion

Hydrochloric acid is naturally present in the stomach, where it helps digestion and limits some microbes.

Safety: Some acids, including concentrated hydrochloric, nitric, and sulfuric acid, can cause serious injury. Never handle them without teacher supervision and proper lab safety equipment.
